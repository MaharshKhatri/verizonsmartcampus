## The theses (one paragraph each)

**A. EcoSlice (seed).** Predict campus occupancy 30 min ahead from bus flows, timetables and 5G density; sleep idle massive-MIMO/MEC; pre-cool buildings; student crowd navigator. *Killer flaw found in Gen 1:* Verizon already runs AI sleep modes (Samsung AI-ESM, 15% average; "over $200 million of energy savings"). In our twin, with the same planner, campus context adds only **~1%** radio energy on ordinary days. The $1.8B "RAN electricity" premise was unsupported.

**B. ZeroRoll (seed).** Phones, buses and delivery bots as a passive RF/SAR swarm to localize faults and self-heal beams. *Flaw:* synthetic-aperture radar from consumer phones is not real; per-device RF data is CPNI; Verizon says "up to 85% of domain issues" are already resolved autonomously. Salvaged idea: crowd-sourced RSRP *aggregates* as one symptom in triage.

**C. PulseMEC (seed).** Route inference and support workloads across MEC nodes by heat and carbon. *Flaw:* small, hard to demo, MEC product status uncertain; no campus customer pain.

**D. The Network Is the Sensor.** Verizon sells privacy-preserving building occupancy (5G counts + the campus's own Wi-Fi + timetable) as a service; it drives occupancy-based HVAC control with shared savings. *Strength:* attacks the biggest controllable cost a campus has (HVAC ≈ 61% of education-building energy; Rutgers ≈ $60M/yr energy) without new sensors, which PNNL identifies as the reason occupancy controls fail to pay off. *Weakness:* Siemens Lambent / Cisco Spaces / Juniper Mist already sell Wi-Fi occupancy analytics.

**E. GridCell VPP.** Enroll cell-site batteries and campus loads in PJM demand response. *Weakness:* batteries are backup for outages (resilience obligations); Verizon program claim unverifiable; little CX.

**F. Sentinel Care.** Zero-touch self-triage for campus/enterprise connectivity tickets. *Weakness:* Verizon already deploys Gemini agents for the majority of inbound consumer contacts; little B2B novelty alone.

**G. SliceGate.** Campus IT requests guaranteed connectivity in plain English; parsed to S-NSSAI/5QI, checked against the crowd forecast, provisioned in seconds (CAMARA QoD / Slice Booking). *Strength:* demo-able, uses Verizon's flagship tech (slicing, Frontline). *Weakness:* revenue per campus is modest without a bigger anchor deal.

**H. CapEx Oracle.** Use the twin to defer small-cell builds. *Weakness:* capex decisions are slow and data-poor at student scale; no CX.

**I. Dorm-to-Door FWA.** Off-campus student housing on FWA/Frontier fiber with AI-guided self-install. Consumer, not campus.

**J. Campus Guardian.** Public-safety slice for RUPD + MEC video analytics. Overlaps 2025–26 winner SafeRoute; privacy-heavy.

**K. Freshman Funnel.** Use network experience to win 18–24-year-old subscribers. Marketing, not engineering.

**L. Carbon-aware edge.** Shift deferrable compute to idle MEC. Small.

## Synthesis: the Grand Unified Thesis, "Circadian"

> **One privacy-safe campus forecast, three paychecks.** Timetable + event calendar + device counts (campus Wi-Fi indoors, Verizon at the stadium and outdoors) → a 30-minute occupancy forecast per building.
> 1. **Campus pays (B2B, Pillar 2 + 3):** occupancy intelligence, ventilation by headcount with comfort-safe setback, demand response and slices, sold by Verizon Business to universities, hospitals and corporate campuses.
> 2. **Verizon saves (Pillar 1 + 2):** event-day energy saving without manual event mode (pre-wake instead of lock-on), and intent-aware triage that avoids about a third of dispatches in simulated fault storms.
> 3. **Students win (Pillar 3):** a crowd and quiet-space navigator, guaranteed service at events, and rooms conditioned when people are actually in them.

What changed because of the red teams: RAN energy was demoted from headline to event-day proof; the B2B product became the money; Wi-Fi became the stated primary indoor sensor; the HVAC claim became "comfort-safe" rather than "the forecast saves kWh"; privacy became an architecture slide, not a footnote.
