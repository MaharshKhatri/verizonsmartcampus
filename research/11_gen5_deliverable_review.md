# Generation 5: fact-check and judge review of the actual deliverables (v1)

Four reviewers read the v1 video script, executive summary and Q&A bible: a fact-checker, a Verizon executive watching video #37 of 40, a non-specialist Vice Provost, and a rival team's captain. The v2 deliverables apply every must-fix and most should-fix items.

## fact-checker (score 127)

I checked 127 claims: 28 in the video script, 53 in the executive summary and 46 in the Q&A bible. About 82 are supported, 27 overstated, 13 unsupported and 5 wrong.

What holds up: every Verizon-document quote is verbatim in research/sources. That includes "Compatibility is determined by your testing ... at time of install", "Failure to verify may result in your alarm not working", the ##21 step and the swap guide's lone "make a test call". The FCC 26-19 para 31 quote is accurate, and the 5 million dialer panels figure is the AICC's own, cited in FCC 26-19 n.110. The CSV gives exactly 1,763 wire centers in 21 states, including PSWYNJPI.

The 417k figure is now confirmed from primary sources. I extracted the RI PUC copy of the WC 26-227 application (Aug 10, 2026): "only approximately 277,000 residential and business legacy voice lines". It also repeats the 46,000 nationwide figure. The 140k in the 8 Frontier states is quoted in research/06, and the two dockets' states do not overlap. research/06 §21 and line 1120 still mark the 277k as unverified, so update them.

The prototype numbers reproduce: 55 tests pass; the SINR threshold is 10.2 dB (CI 8.6-11.5); routing accuracy is 92% and 86.7% (±0.15 perturbation); 5% loss gives 60% delivered within 4 tries; and the 8 s message gives 0.0.

The problems fall into four groups:
1. **The replacement is always a wireless box.** Verizon's own NJ notice and its 26-227 filing list fiber or cable VoIP and mobile service too. The verizon_planning judge said most NJ copper moves to Fios or mobile.
2. **Modelled or simulated results are presented as measured or real.** Examples: "alarm data fails before voice does" (voice quality is not modelled at all), "fails on a real 2-wire line" and "0% of real people" (both are simulation), and the Oct 22-31 bench listed under "what is real today".
3. **Delivery-proof claims the judges already flagged.** These are "one button", "Was the message intact?", "Test reached receiver" and the Q&A #8 kiss-off answer.
4. **Status claims not yet true on Oct 21.** These are "we asked the mentor" and "we asked OIT" (the action plan sends them Oct 21-22, and there is no outreach log), a Facilities co-sign, "Rutgers PROVE Lab" as a named unit, KPIs "Verizon already tracks" (sourced only from a simulated judge persona), and blue lights "off phone lines" (network type unverified).

Smaller errors: the lab receiver also decodes Express 4+1/4+2, not just Contact ID. The signal model uses SINR only, not SINR/RSRQ. The script has about 225 spoken words, not 270. The 8 must-fix items are all wording changes that can land before filming.

**Best line:** EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY_2PAGE.md:8, verbatim from Verizon's own BPC100 guide: alarm and medical compatibility "is determined by your testing ... at time of install", and "Failure to verify may result in your alarm not working". This is the most solidly sourced and persuasive claim in the package, because it states the problem in the sponsor's own words.
