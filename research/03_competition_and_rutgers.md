**Challenge statement (VERIFIED, quoted):** "How can we leverage Verizon's next-gen network to build a smarter, more efficient world and drive real growth? … build a solution that delivers clear, measurable business value … must align with one of Verizon's strategic pillars … We'll be evaluating how well you use our advanced network capabilities to drive scalable innovation and deliver tangible business outcomes."

**Structure (VERIFIED, main-page FAQ)**
* Round 1: concept pitch video. About 15 teams advance.
* Round 2: posters judged by Verizon mentors. About 7 teams advance. Mentor support is provided to Round 2 teams.
* Round 3: final presentation. Three teams go to the Winners Circle.
* ⚠️ **Final-talk length conflicts:** the main-page FAQ says "10 minutes", but the Mar 4, 2027 event page says "polished, 5-minute presentations". Ask the coordinator.
* **Video length:** "Prepare a two-minute video presentation" (Guidelines PDF, uploaded 2025-10, still linked from the 2026 page). Video may be "PowerPoint with voiceover" or a direct-to-camera presentation; AI use is allowed within the guidelines.
* ⚠️ **Demo requirement conflicts:** the 2025-10 Guidelines PDF says "Demonstrations and/or prototypes are not required and will not affect the judging evaluation". The 2026 FAQ says projects "must include a detailed problem statement, a value proposition, and a demonstration of how the solution works". Plan for a lightweight demo.

**Judging criteria (VERIFIED)**
* Main-page FAQ: "innovation, feasibility, impact, and presentation … creative solutions that are well-researched and effectively address a real campus need."
* Poster round (event page): "impact, feasibility, creativity, and presentation quality."
* Rubric Scoring Guide dimensions: **Impact, Feasibility, Creativity, Content, Presentation**. "High" Feasibility means "Real-world, cost-effective solution that can be rapidly prototyped and tested; addresses adoption barriers". "High" Content means "backed by data, research, or real-world validation."
* **Round 1 scorecard (out of 95):**

| Section | Sub-criteria (points) | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Significance /10, Ubiquity ("How many people does it impact?") /10, Criticality /10 | **30** |
| Feasibility | Real-World /5, Cost Effective /5, Scalable /5 | 15 |
| Content | Comprehensive /5, Researched (data/evidence) /5, **Complexity: "incorporation of Verizon network technologies"** /5 | 15 |
| Creativity | Innovative /5 | 5 |
| Presentation | Organized /10, Engaged /10, Clarity /10 | **30** |

**Prizes (VERIFIED):** Final-round teams receive a **$500 stipend** and one-on-one time with Verizon coaches, plus Verizon Mentor Hub access. The **top three teams each receive $3,000**, "to split evenly amongst team members".

## 2. Verizon ↔ Rutgers, and WINLAB / COSMOS / ORBIT

### 2.1 Verizon–Rutgers relationship
* **VERIFIED:** Verizon has run the Smart Campus Competition with Rutgers SoE since 2024-25, plus the 2025 RBS Go-to-Market spin-off. Winner's Circles are held at Verizon's Executive Briefing Center in Basking Ridge, NJ.
* **UNVERIFIED (searched, nothing found):** a Verizon sponsorship of WINLAB, Verizon 5G Ultra Wideband or a Verizon-specific DAS at SHI Stadium, and Verizon as a Rutgers Athletics "official wireless partner". The Athletics partner deals we found were with Jackpocket, Shift4, Nike/BSN and Valley Bank, with no carrier. Ask the Verizon mentors directly.

### 2.2 WINLAB
* Founded **1989**. Address: **671 Route 1 South, North Brunswick, NJ 08902**. **VERIFIED** — <https://www.winlab.rutgers.edu/about/>
* ⚠️ **WINLAB is not on Busch.** It is in North Brunswick, near the OSM "Technology Centre of New Jersey" stop, about 6.4 km (COMPUTED, straight line) from Busch Student Center.
* Research areas: mobile computing, radio resource management, network architectures, programmable testbeds, dynamic spectrum access, security. It has an industry sponsor program (equipment vendors, chipmakers, service providers). **VERIFIED**
* **Roles** (COSMOS team page; page date unknown): Dipankar Raychaudhuri, Director, WINLAB, and COSMOS PI; Narayan Mandayam, Associate Director, WINLAB (ECE); Ivan Seskar, Chief Technologist, WINLAB, and COSMOS Program Director. Co-PIs are at Columbia (Gil Zussman) and NYU (Sundeep Rangan). **VERIFIED** — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/team/>. A January 2025 Rutgers ECE article also calls Seskar "program director of the COSMOS testbed".
* **O-RAN:** Rutgers (Jan 21, 2025) says WINLAB's "O-RAN Test and Integration Centers, one of the few such centers in North America", together with the "indoor ORBIT lab in New Jersey and outdoor COSMOS testbed in New York City", support the $10M NTIA SALSA project (upper-mid-band/FR3 RFIC for O-RAN). **VERIFIED** — <https://ece.rutgers.edu/news/rutgers-receives-ntia-funding-develop-next-generation-wireless-communications-technology>
  * The O-RAN ALLIANCE lists a "North American OTIC in NYC Metro Area/East (COSMOS)". **VERIFIED** — <https://www.o-ran.org/otics>
  * The OTIC is said to be jointly hosted with AT&T (2023 O-RAN press release). **SNIPPET** — <https://o-ran.org/press-releases/o-ran-alliance-welcomes-four-new-otics-in-north-america>
  * WINLAB + ONF received a $1.97M Wireless Innovation Fund award for 5G O-RAN energy efficiency. **SNIPPET**

### 2.3 COSMOS and ORBIT (the practical test infrastructure)
* **COSMOS** is an NSF **PAWR** testbed (award CNS-1827923) run by Rutgers WINLAB, Columbia and NYU. Its outdoor footprint is about 1 sq mi of **West Harlem**, NYC. **VERIFIED** — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/>
* **ORBIT was folded into COSMOS.** The orbit-lab.org websites were retired on **1 August 2026**. "ORBIT itself did not go away": the ORBIT 20×20 indoor grid and its sandboxes are now COSMOS domains, run from the same portal and scheduler. **VERIFIED** — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/legacy>
* **What is available** (VERIFIED — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/>, <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/hardware>, <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/hardware/domains>):
  * **SDRs:** USRP X310, N310, X410, B210, N210 and 2974.
  * **28 GHz mmWave:** IBM PAAM phased arrays.
  * **O-RAN / 5G:** "O-RAN-compliant 7.2x O-RUs at multiple NYC sites plus programmable srsRAN / OpenAirInterface stacks running on edge servers". There is an O-RU pool of 9 units from 5 vendors (Benetel, Foxconn, LiteOn, Jabil, Corning).
  * **Edge compute:** Intel Xeon servers and **NVIDIA A100 80 GB** GPUs.
  * Optical x-haul and an FR3 (Pi-Radio) testbed.
* **Domains relevant to Rutgers NB:**

| Domain | Location | What it offers |
|---|---|---|
| **Grid** | WINLAB, North Brunswick | Legacy ORBIT 20×20 grid: "several hundred programmable nodes, 23 N210 and 22 X310 radios" |
| **Sandboxes** | WINLAB (NJ) | Small two-node environments, e.g. sb5 (B210), sb6 (X310), sb4 (attenuator matrix), sb9 (FPGA/GPU) |
| **Weeks** | **Weeks Hall (NJAMI), Busch campus, Piscataway** | "Pilot 5G Factory in Weeks Hall", a Siemens + Rutgers collaboration. Low-latency, high-reliability **private 5G with edge computing**. Includes a COSMOS large-node sector, a Simcom SIM8200 5G-modem node, 4 compute servers and a Jetson Xavier with cameras ([cosmos_weeks page](https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/hardware/domains/cosmos_weeks)). **This is real 5G test infrastructure on Busch, next to the competition venue.** |
| **Minicity** | ORBIT room, WINLAB | Scale-model smart intersection (modeled on 120th St × Amsterdam Ave, NYC) with RSU nodes, cameras, lidar, 5 GHz V2X and a model car |

  The wiki home also mentions a smart-intersection sandbox "at the Rutgers Livingston campus intersection". That conflicts with the Minicity page, so it is **UNVERIFIED**.
* **Access** (VERIFIED — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/getting-started>):
  * Register on the COSMOS portal, "pick your group … then have your **PI approve** the request". You therefore need a Rutgers faculty sponsor.
  * Reservations are self-service through the scheduler: 2 h maximum on the Grid, up to 6 h on COSMOS domains, auto-approved, with fairness priority for lower 28-day usage. Access is by SSH to a per-domain console.
* **Student precedent:** WINLAB's Summer Internship includes undergraduate projects such as "O-RAN network energy management" (2026), "Digital Twin for Network Energy Consumption" and "5G Edge Cloud Application" (2025), and "CityOS" (2025-26). **VERIFIED** — <https://www.cosmos-lab.org/wiki/public/summer>
