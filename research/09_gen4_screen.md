Each persona read the briefing, the research digest, the founder's contender (`research/08_gen4_founder_contender.md`) and all ideas (`research/07_gen4_ideas.md`). Scores are out of 95 on the official rubric.

## Rutgers CIO + Dean of Engineering

**Founder contender: 74/95.** This is the right problem and the strongest Verizon-specific evidence in the pool. As written it is too many products for 18 days and 2 minutes. Verizon's own documents carry the case: "Compatibility is determined by your testing ... at time of install", the ##21 step, "Failure to verify may result in your alarm not working", 3 AA batteries, and a swap guide that never mentions alarms. Combine that with the FCC deleting 63.602 and the safe-use notices on Oct 15 and you have the best Content hook any team will have. The tagline "when the regulator stops checking, the carrier that proves wins" is excellent. Verizon's network-planning judges will see that it speaks to dispatch cost and copper-exit speed.

The problems:
- v3/v4 is really three products: install-time PROVE, nightly PROVE and Cutover Watch. They span five device classes (voice, alarm, medical, fax, elevator) plus three human studies: the HELP audit, OLLI and the household panel. That will not fit a 2-minute video, and the Dean and Vice Provost will get lost in docket numbers.
- Rutgers is honestly the proving ground, not the customer. I respect the honesty, but as CIO I will ask what Rutgers gets. The answer has to be an elevator WHO ANSWERS pilot and a PROVE Lab co-signed by OIT and Facilities. Never frame it as finding faults in our lines.
- The weakness the founder lists himself is the killer Q&A question: "our technicians already test at install, and MDM sees the battery."
- Nothing yet shows a real failure measured on Verizon's real box; the prototype is simulation only. Without one measured red line on the receipt (swap drops ##21, SIA fails, AAs absent, basement VoLTE lost), it is a hypothesis.
- Medical-alert tests need monitoring-company permission.
- Do not put Rutgers pins on the retiring wire-center map, do not say Dec 10, and do not say the FCC approved anything.

Make Plug-and-PROVE the only hero of the video: one BPC100, one button, one receipt, "TRUCK NOT NEEDED". Put battery, signal at the spot and the ##21/kiss-off check on that receipt. Hold WHO ANSWERS, with its checks run from the network side and no relay on the line, for the 10-minute final as the Rutgers pilot. Cutover Watch stays a single roadmap slide. With that focus and one measured failure it reaches about 80.

## Presentation coach

**Founder contender: 72/95.** The research behind PROVE IT is the strongest in the pool, but the story is weaker than any other top-five entry. The facts are current, verified and Verizon-specific: the FCC 26-19 deletions took effect Oct 15, the business-line exit (WC 26-228) drew no pause, and Verizon's own BPC100 documents contradict each other. The production path (firmware via Verizon MDM) is real, and the money comes from truck rolls, a cost line the GN&T judges own. The problem is that v4 is two pitches stapled together. One is a home box that prints a receipt (national scale, cost line). The other is a Rutgers elevator 'who answers' audit (campus, emotional). In a 2-minute video a non-specialist judge hears 'Contact ID kiss-off, ##21, T.30 fax, RSRQ, A17.1 45 s' and does not understand the idea within 20 seconds. The draft also says outright that Rutgers is 'the proving ground, not the customer'. That is a bad line in a Smart Campus contest where the Dean, the CIO and the Vice Provost vote. Past winners each had one object, one familiar Rutgers place and one pain a judge felt immediately. This draft has five objects and no place. There are also substance risks. Verizon may already verify installs. The $250 truck-roll figure and the 60% pass share are assumptions. Medical-alert tests need a monitoring company's permission. Rutgers' own copper exposure is probably small. As written it scores about 72/95. Fix: use one visual grammar, the PROVE receipt. Open on a Rutgers elevator: someone presses HELP, the code-required line LED stays green, and the receipt prints 'ANSWERED BY: VOICEMAIL. FAIL.' Then show 'the same button, on the box Verizon puts in a million homes', with the receipt reading 'ALARM ok / BATTERY none / TRUCK NOT NEEDED'. Cut fax, TTY, medical, the in-car acoustic sensor and the 1,763-wire-center map from the video, and keep them for the poster and Q&A. Frame it as 'the FCC stopped checking; Verizon proves it anyway', never as a gotcha. Never say 'Dec 10' or 'the FCC approved'. With that edit it is about 78 and our strongest entry.

## Verizon GN&T leadership

**Founder contender: 74/95.** FOUNDER belongs at #1, but as written it is a merger memo, not a pitch. It has six test types, two customer segments (Voice Connect homes and SMBs nationally; Rutgers elevators), two hardware forms (BPC100 routine and machine-room unit), a lab, a map and a ledger. A 2-minute video can carry one hero.

**What makes it the best in the pool.** It is built on Verizon's own documents, which no other LLM-assisted team will assemble:
- Voice Connect 2nd Gen is literally the BPC100 guide.
- "Compatibility is determined by your testing ... at time of install."
- The ##21 alarm-number step, and "Failure to verify may result in your alarm not working."
- The mailed swap guide mentions alarms zero times.
- Verizon has ~46k fixed-wireless POTS-replacement customers against ~140k lines in 8 Frontier states alone.

Add the Oct 15, 2026 deletion of 63.602 and the safe-use notices, and it speaks directly to a cost line the GN&T judges own: technician dispatches and install capacity.

**What will cost points:**
1. **"We already do this."** A Verizon spokeswoman says "teams ... come out and test everything before that copper is disconnected." Until the bench shows one failure Verizon's current process misses (for example, ##21 registrations lost on the mailed swap, SIA or medical-alert failures, or the battery at 5 C against the rated 23 h), a Verizon judge can dismiss the whole thing in one sentence.
2. **The demo receipt contradicts Verizon's partner.** It prints "MEDICAL ALERT ok", while Inseego's own BPC100 guide says "Calls to Medical or Life Alert systems" cannot be made. Either measure it, or make the contradiction the point (the Rutgers PROVE Lab resolves it).
3. **The money is thin and assumption-built.** $15M one-time is a rounding error at Verizon. Pitch install capacity (installs must scale several-fold before the exits) and approval velocity, not dollars.
4. **Gotcha optics.** "Verizon's box fails silently" must be framed as "Verizon did what the FCC encouraged."
5. **The campus is the proving ground, not the customer.** Fire communicators (2020) and blue lights (2023) are already off copper, and Gate A is unresolved. The elevator anchor is what earns "Smart Campus" with the Dean and CIO, so it must survive as a 20-second Rutgers-data beat.

**Cut from the video:**
- the in-car two-transducer test as lead (2N, SafeLine and the Esse-Ti 2007 patent are prior art);
- Deaf text mode;
- the FIND classifier (replace it with the NOTICE ME consent registry);
- the Dec 10 countdown;
- any "FCC approved";
- the 2.8-day fleet model and the 5%-loss/60%-delivered model presented as findings (they are models);
- Ooma's "50% don't work" stated as fact (it is a vendor claim).

**Recommended shape:**
- Video: about 75 s on the box hero (plug in, press PROVE, receipt, swap per Verizon's guide, PANIC, no kiss-off), 20 s on WHO ANSWERS with a real Rutgers HELP-audit number, and 15 s on the 1,763-wire-center Cutover Watch map.
- Poster: the PROVE Lab matrix.
- Finals: SILENT AFTER CUTOVER as the "every line, no box" network tier.

**Uniqueness:** about 8/10. Copper and POTS could occur to a few research-agent teams. The ##21 detail, the A17.1 2.27.1.1.6(a) clause and the bench numbers will not.
