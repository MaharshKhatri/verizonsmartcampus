**Prepared:** 2026-10-20 (as of this date, Verizon has **not** yet reported Q3 2026. It reports on **Mon Oct 26, 2026**, with the release at 7:00 a.m. ET; [Verizon notice](https://www.verizon.com/about/news/verizon-q3-2026-earnings-report-date)).
**Method:** I pulled primary documents directly from SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000732712) and Verizon Investor Relations: the FY2025 10-K, the Q1/Q2 2026 10-Qs, the earnings-release exhibits (EX-99), the 8-Ks, the LSEG call transcripts posted by Verizon IR, the EY-assured ESG schedule and the green bond report. I used secondary sources only where no primary source exists, and I label them as secondary.
**Rule used:** Every number below has a source link. "UNVERIFIABLE" means I could not find a credible source, so **do not say it on stage**.

> All $ figures are US$ millions unless noted. "Adj." = non-GAAP as defined by Verizon.

### Electricity spend estimate (my estimate, not a Verizon disclosure)
Verizon does not disclose its electricity bill. Method: grid electricity (MWh) × average EIA retail price.

- 2025 grid electricity = 41,076,950 GJ ÷ 3.6 × 89.8% = **10,246,417 MWh**.
  - Cross-check: 66.3% ÷ 73.9% = 89.7% of energy is electricity, which gives 10,236,813 MWh.
- EIA 2025 annual average retail prices ([EIA API v2, retail-sales](https://api.eia.gov/v2/electricity/retail-sales/); same series as the [EIA Electricity Data Browser](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/browser/)): US commercial **13.41¢/kWh**, US industrial **8.62¢/kWh**.

| Price assumption | ¢/kWh | Implied annual electricity spend |
|---|---:|---:|
| US industrial avg 2025 | 8.62 | **$0.88B** |
| Midpoint of industrial and commercial | 11.02 | **$1.13B** |
| US commercial avg 2025 | 13.41 | **$1.37B** |
| US commercial YTD Jul-2026 | 13.97 | $1.43B |
| NJ commercial 2025 (upper-bound sensitivity only) | 16.63 | $1.70B |

**Conclusion:** Verizon's total electricity spend is most plausibly **~$0.9–1.4B per year**. Use "roughly $1 billion+" if you need a round number, and label it an estimate.

**Checking the claim "~75% of network electricity is RAN, ~$1.8B/yr":**
- The RAN share is an industry figure, not a Verizon one:
  - [GSMA Intelligence](https://www.gsmaintelligence.com/event/going-green-can-5g-be-energy-efficient/): "73% of the energy consumption of participating operators is consumed on the radio access network (RAN)".
  - [Ericsson Technology Review, 2026](https://www.ericsson.com/49cbbd/assets/local/reports-papers/ericsson-technology-review/docs/2026/breaking-the-energy-curve-in-ran.pdf): "According to GSMA Intelligence, roughly 80 percent of the energy used by mobile networks is consumed in the RAN". The same paper says radios are "about 92 percent" of radio-unit plus baseband energy.
  - Both figures describe **mobile** networks. Verizon's electricity also powers wireline (Fios/central offices), data centers and buildings, so the RAN share of Verizon's *total* is probably lower.
- Upper-bound math: 73–80% × $0.88–1.37B = **$0.65–1.10B per year**.
- $1.8B would require ~17.6¢/kWh on *all* Verizon electricity, or ~23.4¢/kWh on a 75% RAN slice. That is above any national C&I average. **Do not use $1.8B.**
- Sanity check on Schulman's "$200M of energy savings" from AI: that equals ~15–23% of the estimated $0.9–1.4B bill. This is in the same range as the 15% average savings Verizon reported in the Samsung AI-ESM field tests, but he did not say whether the $200M is annual or cumulative.

**Other Verizon energy initiatives** ([2025 TCFD Report](https://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/Verizon-2025-TCFD-Report.pdf)):
- "We are utilizing AI and machine learning capabilities to drive energy efficiency initiatives across Verizon."
- In the network: "utilizing lower power modes and developing equipment sleep features", installing high-temperature batteries, upgrading HVAC, and decommissioning legacy copper switches.
- In the fleet: "optimized vehicle dispatching technology and automation to minimize the miles driven… and **reduce the number of truck rolls needed for Fios dispatches**."
- On-site generation: 38.6 MW installed since 2013 ([sustainability page](https://www.verizon.com/about/responsibility/sustainability)).
- Backup power: batteries at all macro sites, plus generators at most of them ([2024 RB Update](https://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2024-Responsible-Business-Report.pdf)).
- **Not verifiable:** any Verizon–Enersponse demand-response program. I found no source.
