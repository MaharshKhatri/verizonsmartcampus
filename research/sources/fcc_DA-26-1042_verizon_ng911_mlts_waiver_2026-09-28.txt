I.      INTRODUCTION
         1.      In this Order, the Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau (Bureau) conditionally
grants a limited waiver to Verizon of section 9.30(a)(1) of the Commission’s rules,1 which establishes
deadlines for originating service providers (OSPs) to implement Phase 1 of the transition to Next
Generation 911 (NG911).2 Verizon requests an extension of the six-month deadline to comply with Phase
1 requirements for its wireless and wireline services in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts and for its
wireline service in other jurisdictions that filed Phase 1 requests prior to January 31, 2026. Verizon
requests the waiver to allow it additional time to address technical challenges it encountered with certain
multi-line telephone system (MLTS) customers when implementing Phase 1 in Massachusetts.
         2.       For the reasons set forth below and subject to the conditions outlined herein, we grant
Verizon a limited extension of the deadline for Phase 1 implementation of its wireline services in
Massachusetts and certain other jurisdictions. (We identify the specific jurisdictions covered by the
waiver in the Appendix.) We also dismiss as moot Verizon’s request to extend the Phase 1 deadline for
its wireless service in Massachusetts, as both Verizon and the Massachusetts State 911 Department
(Massachusetts) agree that Verizon Wireless achieved compliance with Phase 1 requirements prior to
submitting its waiver request.

two phases.3 Since March 2025, 911 Authorities who meet specific NG911 readiness thresholds have
been able to submit requests to OSPs to begin delivering 911 traffic in Internet Protocol (IP) format.4 In
Phase 1, OSPs must commence delivery of 911 traffic in IP-based Session Initiation Protocol (SIP)

when initiating first time deployments.11 Recognizing that initial NG911 transitions “may take longer as

III.      DISCUSSION
         17.     Waiver standard. Section 1.3 of the Commission’s rules provides that the Commission
may “on its own motion or on petition” waive a rule “for good cause shown, in whole or in part, at any
time.”61 The Commission may find that the “good cause” standard is met only if: (1) “special
circumstances warrant a deviation from the general rule”; and (2) “such deviation will serve the public
interest.”62 Section 1.925(b)(3) of the rules provides that the Commission may grant a waiver when either
(i) the underlying purpose of the rule(s) “would not be served or would be frustrated by application to the
instant case,” and a grant of the requested waiver would be in the public interest, or (ii) in view of unique
or unusual factual circumstances of the instant case, application of the rule(s) would be “inequitable,
unduly burdensome or contrary to the public interest, or the applicant has no reasonable alternative.”63
The waiver standard under these two rule sections is “virtually identical.”64 With respect to NG911
implementations, the Commission stated in the NG911 Transition Order that the waiver process is
available for “providers facing extraordinary circumstances,” particularly in the context of OSPs
connecting with ESInets and NGCS service providers for the first time.65 After consideration of the
Verizon Petition and the record in this proceeding, we find that good cause exists under the special
circumstances of this case and that it is in the public interest to grant Verizon a limited waiver of the
Phase 1 deadline, subject to the conditions set forth below.
         18.      Verizon has shown special circumstances warranting a waiver. We find that Verizon has
shown special circumstances that warrant a waiver in this instance. On March 25, 2025, the NG911
transition rules took effect authorizing 911 Authorities to submit Phase 1 and Phase 2 requests, thereby
triggering implementation deadlines for OSPs.66 Massachusetts filed its Phase 1 request 10 days later, on
April 4, 2025. Accordingly, Verizon’s implementation of Phase 1 in Massachusetts was one of its earliest
wireline NG911 deployments. As Verizon initiated deployment in Massachusetts, its connectivity testing
uncovered the issue involving location information for MLTS using third-party PS/ALI service and the

implementation variables that 911 Authorities, PSAPs and OSPs may encounter, especially in the
‘onboarding phase’ of initial deployments.”69
         20.      We disagree with the suggestion that Verizon should have foreseen the PS/ALI issue
earlier because of its prior business decision not to provide its own PS/ALI service.70 Verizon was free to
make this business decision, and the record does not indicate that Verizon should have anticipated at that
time that its decision might impact future transition to NG911. In fact, Verizon appears to have been the
first OSP to discover the impact of this issue on the NG911 transition, and Verizon and other commenters
have since recognized that the issue extends beyond Massachusetts and may affect any OSP with
customers who have chosen to use a PS/ALI service.71 As Intrado notes, “No other carriers, 9-1-1
vendors, or industry experts identified this architectural interaction between legacy PS/ALI products and
Phase 1 SIP connectivity until Verizon’s January 2026 [FOA] testing revealed it.”72

a limited Phase 1 extension serves the public interest because resolving the PS/ALI issue will lead to

national relay center. We agree with Massachusetts that routing 911 calls to a relay center should be
minimized because it can cause a delay in police, fire, and ambulance response times.73 But we also find
that once Verizon identified the MLTS PS/ALI issue, it responded in a manner that was reasonable and
proactive. Verizon promptly brought the issue to the attention of Massachusetts, Allerium, the
Commission, other OSPs, and 911 Authorities. Verizon also worked with Intrado to develop a solution

         23.      Further, we believe the waiver will not unduly delay the transition to NG911 or
inappropriately shift costs to 911 Authorities, as some commenters assert.78 To the contrary, by
implementing a solution to the PS/ALI issue before deploying NG911 in a live environment, Verizon will
be able to more efficiently deploy both Phase 1 and Phase 2 for MLTS not only in Massachusetts but also
in other jurisdictions. In addition, Verizon has raised awareness of the MLTS issue with other OSPs and
911 Authorities so that they can address it as well without necessarily needing Phase 1 or Phase 2
extensions.
         24.     We emphasize that the extension of time granted to Verizon is limited to the situation and
facts that Verizon has presented. If other OSPs submit waiver requests raising similar issues, we will
evaluate such requests based on their specific circumstances, which may differ from Verizon’s. We also
note that since Verizon has raised awareness of the MLTS PS/ALI issue among OSPs and in the public
record, we consider all OSPs nationwide to be on notice of the need to assess whether they will face
similar issues when deploying Phase 1 (or Phase 2) for wireline services in all jurisdictions they serve.
Wireline OSPs should therefore promptly examine whether they have TDM-based MLTS customers
using PS/ALI services and begin efforts to obtain location information for such customers, even if they
have not yet received a Phase 1 or Phase 2 request. Similarly, MLTS operators and managers using TDM
systems and third-party PS/ALI services should work with their OSPs to help ensure that MLTS 911 calls
will be routed successfully in Phase 1 and Phase 2.

        28.       We agree with Massachusetts on the importance of ensuring that Verizon takes all
necessary actions to resolve the MLTS PS/ALI issue as quickly and comprehensively as possible. We

information, with updates, to Verizon on an ongoing basis. This condition will help to ensure that
Verizon completes the process of notifying its MLTS PS/ALI customers in a timely manner. It also
responds to Massachusetts’ objection that the notice Verizon has provided to its customers concerning the
PS/ALI issue has not been adequate.84 We agree with Massachusetts on the importance of ensuring that
notice of this issue reaches as many of Verizon’s MLTS PS/ALI customers as possible and accordingly
require Verizon to use its best efforts to notify these customers individually.
