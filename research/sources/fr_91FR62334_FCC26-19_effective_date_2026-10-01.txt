AGENCY: National Marine Fisheries

Service (NMFS), National Oceanic and
Atmospheric Administration (NOAA),
Commerce.
ACTION: Final rule.
SUMMARY: NMFS is modifying the

annual catch limit (ACL) and
accountability measures (AM) for
Mariana bottomfish management unit
species (MUS) in the Guam
Management Subarea (the fishery). The
rule increases the ACL from 31,000
pounds (lb) (14,061 kilograms (kg)) to
34,500 lb (15,649 kg), replaces the inseason AM with a post-season overage
adjustment if the average catch from the
most recent 3 years exceeds the ACL,
and removes the higher performance
standard that closes the fishery in
Federal waters for any overage. The rule
is based on the best available scientific,
commercial, and other information
about the fishery, and supports
rebuilding of the fishery.
DATES: The final rule is effective
November 2, 2026.

Ecosystem Plan for the Mariana
Archipelago (FEP) are available from the
Western Pacific Fishery Management
Council (Council), 1164 Bishop St.,
Suite 1400, Honolulu, HI 96813, tel.
808–522–8220, fax 808–522–8226, or
https://www.wpcouncil.org.

on: (1) a 2024 stock assessment update
for Mariana bottomfish MUS in Guam;
(2) consideration of the best scientific,
commercial, and other information
about the fishery; (3) a review from the
Council’s Scientific and Statistical
Committee; and (4) input from the
public. Accordingly, NMFS implements
this action under section 303(c) and
section 304(b) of the Magnuson-Stevens
Act.
The fishery targets 13 species of
bottomfish that are managed as a multispecies stock complex. Accordingly,
stock status and management measures
such as ACLs and AMs are applied to
the stock complex rather than to
individual species. NMFS determined
the stock complex was overfished but
not experiencing overfishing (85 FR
26940, May 6, 2020), and it has been
subject to a rebuilding plan with an ACL
of 31,000 lb (14,061 kg) since 2022 (87
FR 9271, February 18, 2022). An
updated stock assessment completed in
2024 by the NMFS Pacific Islands
Fisheries Science Center found that the
stock was no longer overfished and
continued to not experience overfishing.
However, while stock biomass had
increased above the ‘‘minimum stock
size threshold’’ for the overfished
condition, it had not increased to the
biomass that would produce maximum
sustainable yield. This means the stock
is not yet fully rebuilt, so NMFS and the
Council continue to manage the fishery
under the rebuilding plan with a target
time to rebuild by 2031. The stock
assessment update also provided new
projections showing the stock could
support moderately larger catches and
still rebuild by 2031.
