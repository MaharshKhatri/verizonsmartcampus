Through this FCC filing, the small and shrinking number of customers still using legacy copper lines in Florida
will be transitioned to modern networks more suitable for today’s communications needs. Our filing represents
an important step in a carefully managed technology transition reflecting a fundamental evolution in the
communications service consumers demand and how it’s delivered. As we move from legacy copper networks
to modern technologies, no customers will be left behind.

For generations, copper networks played an essential role in connecting communities, families, businesses,
and public institutions. Recognizing the importance of that history, we are managing this transition with great
care. At the same time, consumer demand has changed dramatically. Today, the vast majority of customers in
Florida choose faster and more reliable fiber and wireless services.

Based on FCC reporting as of June 30, 2025, only 2% of households in Florida still use legacy "switched
access” lines (copper landlines) while nearly all households have already transitioned to wireless or fiber voice
services to meet their calling needs. 1

Maintaining an aging, parallel copper network for a small and shrinking number of users diverts crucial
resources away from future investment. Copper infrastructure is many decades old, highly vulnerable to severe
weather, and an increasing target for theft and vandalism. This outdated network is not capable of supporting
the robust, data-intensive uses that today’s customers demand. Every dollar spent patching this obsolete
system is a dollar diverted from building the robust, modern networks that residents actually use. Transitioning
to modern technologies will allow Verizon to improve reliability, reduce unnecessary network duplication, lower
environmental impacts, and recycle valuable materials for productive use.

Most importantly, no customer will be left behind in this transition. Verizon will provide ample notice to our
remaining copper-served customers and work directly with them to identify reliable replacement options. These
options will include service over fiber optic lines where available or home phone replacement services that
preserve a familiar landline experience using existing handsets while operating over Verizon’s award-winning

               1.      Verizon’s Network Performance

       Under the “network performance” prong of the Adequate Replacement Test, an applicant

seeking to discontinue service can make “a demonstration, based on the totality of the

circumstances,” that the network on which a replacement service will run provides substantially

similar performance and availability. 17 The totality of the circumstances demonstrates that users

can rely on Verizon’s mobile wireless network for mobile wireless service and fixed wireless

POTS voice replacement services to replace the legacy voice services Verizon seeks to

discontinue. Verizon offers a 4G LTE and 5G NR (“New Radio”) mobile broadband and voice

service in these eight states: that service is available and qualifies as a legacy POTS replacement

service at the vast majority of locations in Verizon’s service areas in Florida, Indiana, Minnesota,

Nebraska, Nevada, North Carolina, Texas, and Wisconsin. Verizon seeks to discontinue copper¬

based service at these locations. This same mobile wireless network enables Verizon’s fixed

wireless POTS voice replacement services. Voice traffic carried over Verizon’s wireless network

— whether mobile voice service or fixed wireless POTS voice services — does not travel over

the public internet but, instead, travels on Verizon’s private IP network. And, for these services,

voice calls are transmitted using VoLTE — that is, VoIP over LTE, which involves transmission

of “packetized” data.

        While the voice communications are packetized, they are not transported over the public

internet like other non-voice IP packets carried over Verizon’s wireless network. Instead,

Verizon’s wireless voice services use a secure Verizon network designed and managed to reduce

the possibility of congestion, and to mitigate packet loss and delay. Verizon applies a quality-of-

service class identifier (QCI) to VoLTE traffic that gives it priority over internet traffic.

Accordingly, the latency and packet loss for VoLTE calls — including mobile wireless calls and

fixed wireless legacy voice replacement calls — will be no worse, and often better, than those

measured for internet traffic traversing the same parts of the network at the same time.

       In addition, as part of its ordinary course of operations, Verizon evaluates and reports

Mobile Wireless Services

Verizon mobile wireless voice service does not provide line power to keep it operational
during an extended electrical power outage, beyond a single charge of the mobile phone
battery. However, Verizon’s mobile wireless service does not require a backup battery to
remain operational during electric power outages of several hours or less.
